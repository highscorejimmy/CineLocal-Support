Support

Answers for CineLocal 2.8, plus how to get in touch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does CineLocal show ads or ask to track me?

No. Version 2.8 does not include an advertising SDK, does not show Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt, and does not use the advertising identifier (IDFA). Details are in the Privacy Policy.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-in and no CineLocal server that stores your library. Videos you import stay in the app on your device.

What is sent when CineLocal looks up a movie?

The app guesses a title and year from the file name. It sends that text to Apple's iTunes Search service at itunes.apple.com, then downloads the poster from the image address Apple returns. The video file is not uploaded. If you have saved a TMDB key, the same title and year, plus that key, go to The Movie Database first. If you have saved an OMDb key, the matched title and year (or an IMDb id) go to OMDb for Rotten Tomatoes scores. The full list is in the Privacy Policy.

Do I need a TMDB or OMDb API key?

No. Without those keys, lookups use Apple's movie search. A TMDB key in Settings turns on The Movie Database for import and refresh. An OMDb key turns on Rotten Tomatoes scores. TMDB keys are created at themoviedb.org. OMDb keys are requested at omdbapi.com.

Do Watch Party, Nearby Stream, or device copy use a server?

No CineLocal server. Those features discover a nearby device on your local network and connect to it directly. The other device can see your device name, a shortened movie title, a private network address, a join code, and a session token. Copying or streaming sends the video to that device, not to the internet through CineLocal. Watch Party streaming in this version uses MP4, M4V, or MOV.

Can I play movies from a NAS?

Yes, from an SMB share you add. The app sends the username and password you enter to that host only. It does not speak WebDAV or Plex.

What video formats play?

MP4, M4V, and MOV play with Apple's player, which also supports AirPlay and Picture in Picture. Other containers, including many MKV files, play with VLCKit inside the app. A file on an SMB share also uses VLCKit.

Where are my movies stored?

Imported videos and posters are copied into the app's storage on your device. They are not uploaded to a CineLocal server. After import, playback does not need a network connection.

Metadata didn't match my movie. What should I do?

Rename the file so the title is readable, for example Inception (2010).mkv or The Matrix 1999.mp4. Open the movie and refresh its details. You can also search for a different poster; that search uses Apple's movie catalog.

How do I delete a movie?

Open the movie and use delete. That removes the video file, the cached poster, and the library entry from the device.

Troubleshooting

Video won't play

If both Apple's player and VLCKit fail, the file may use a codec this build cannot decode. Convert it to MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio, then import it again.

No poster or details found

The lookup needs an internet connection. Check the file name, then refresh from the movie screen. If you use a TMDB or OMDb key, confirm it was saved in Settings. Clearing the key falls back to Apple's search.

A nearby device does not appear

Both devices need CineLocal, the same Wi-Fi, and Local Network permission for CineLocal. You can also join with the address and code shown on the host.

Import seems slow

Large files take time to copy into the app, especially from a network share. Copying from storage on the device is faster.

Still need help?

Open a request on GitHub Issues. Include your iOS version, device model, and what you expected to happen. Please do not attach the video file.
